\begin{abstract}
This paper presents a systematic comparative benchmark and an ASR-assisted audio-linguistic fusion method for Vietnamese Speech Emotion Recognition (SER) on the ViSEC dataset. We evaluate three primary classification pipelines representing distinct paradigms: handcrafted features with classical ML (MFCC + RandomForest), end-to-end deep learning (ECAPA-TDNN (simplified implementation)), and a proposed dual-stream hybrid fusion system (DFAT) combining WavLM acoustic embeddings with PhoBERT linguistic embeddings derived via Whisper ASR. Four additional secondary baselines are included for comprehensive comparison. All experiments follow a strict leak-free protocol with a fixed speaker-independent Train ($\approx$ 80\%) / Validation ($\approx$ 10\%) / Test ($\approx$ 10\%) split. Classical ML models are evaluated over 5 random seeds to report mean $\pm$ standard deviation. On the held-out test set (528 samples), DFAT Late Fusion Ensemble achieves the best performance with a weighted F1-score of 0.7176 and accuracy of 71.78\%, outperforming the best baseline MFCC+RandomForest (weighted F1 0.6475) and ECAPA-TDNN-based baseline (weighted F1 0.6137) by significant margins. Ablation studies confirm that both acoustic and linguistic streams contribute to DFAT's superiority, with the linguistic stream providing the largest gains for acoustically ambiguous classes.
\end{abstract}

\section{\textbf{Introduction}}
Speech emotion recognition (SER) is a critical task in human-computer interaction, sentiment analysis, call center analytics, and mental health monitoring. Unlike traditional speech recognition (which focuses on what is said), SER aims to identify the emotional state conveyed in a person's voice. Emotions in speech are characterized by distinctive acoustic patterns: frequency content, energy distribution, temporal dynamics, and spectral shapes differ measurably between emotions such as anger, happiness, neutrality, and sadness.

Traditional approaches to SER often rely on hand-crafted acoustic features (e.g., MFCC, prosody), which require domain expertise and can miss subtle patterns. Modern deep learning methods, particularly those using pre-trained speech models such as WavLM~\cite{wavlm} and specialized architectures like ECAPA-TDNN~\cite{ecapa}, have shown superior performance by automatically learning emotion-discriminative representations from raw audio or mel-spectrograms. More recently, hybrid approaches that combine acoustic and linguistic features have emerged as promising directions~\cite{ser_multimodal}.

This work presents a systematic evaluation of multiple SER approaches on Vietnamese speech, comparing feature-based classical methods with contemporary deep learning techniques and a proposed ASR-assisted audio-linguistic fusion system. We address the following research questions:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{RQ1}: How do handcrafted MFCC features compare with end-to-end deep learning (ECAPA-TDNN-based acoustic baseline) for Vietnamese SER on ViSEC?
    \item \textbf{RQ2}: Does ASR-assisted audio-linguistic fusion improve emotion recognition compared to audio-only approaches?
    \item \textbf{RQ3}: Which component of DFAT contributes most to its performance --- the acoustic stream, the linguistic stream, or the fusion strategy?
    \item \textbf{RQ4}: What is the trade-off between recognition performance and computational cost across all evaluated methods?
\end{itemize}

\section{\textbf{Related Work}}

\subsection{Traditional SER}
Classical SER systems typically extract hand-crafted features such as MFCCs, pitch, energy, and prosodic features, then apply machine learning classifiers like SVM~\cite{ser_survey}, Random Forest, or XGBoost~\cite{xgboost}. While interpretable and computationally efficient, these approaches are limited by the quality of manual feature engineering.

\subsection{Self-Supervised Speech Representations}
Recent advances in self-supervised learning have produced powerful speech representations. Models such as Wav2Vec 2.0~\cite{wav2vec2}, HuBERT~\cite{hubert}, and WavLM~\cite{wavlm} learn contextualized embeddings from large-scale unlabeled speech data. These representations have shown strong transfer to downstream tasks including SER, often outperforming hand-crafted features.

\subsection{ECAPA-TDNN}
ECAPA-TDNN~\cite{ecapa} is a specialized architecture combining Time-Delay Neural Networks with Squeeze-and-Excitation blocks and multi-scale feature aggregation. Originally designed for speaker verification, it has been successfully adapted for emotion recognition tasks.

\subsection{Multimodal and Fusion-Based SER}
Multimodal SER approaches combine multiple signal modalities (audio, text, video) to improve recognition accuracy~\cite{ser_multimodal}. In this work, we use Whisper~\cite{whisper} encoder embeddings as ASR-derived linguistic features. Since the text modality is inferred from audio via ASR rather than being an independent source, we characterize our approach as \emph{ASR-assisted audio-linguistic fusion} rather than true multimodal learning.

\section{\textbf{Dataset: ViSEC}}

The Vietnamese Speech Emotion Corpus (ViSEC)~\cite{visec} is publicly available on Hugging Face and contains 5,280 audio utterances from 147 speakers. Each utterance is a single-channel, 16 kHz WAV file annotated with one of four emotion labels.

The class distribution exhibits mild imbalance (Neutral-to-Sad ratio $\approx 1.4\times$). Speaker distribution is highly long-tailed: Speaker 0 contributes 2,217 samples (42\%), while many speakers contribute only 1--2 samples. To ensure robust evaluation and avoid speaker leakage (where models learn speaker identity instead of emotion), we implemented a greedy speaker-allocation algorithm. This guarantees a strict speaker-independent split across the Train/Val/Test sets.

\subsection{Split Protocol}
\label{sec:split_protocol}
We use a fixed speaker-independent split: approx. 80\% Train (4,227), 10\% Validation (525), 10\% Test (528), generated once via a greedy allocation strategy and stored in a manifest file (\texttt{split\_manifest.json}). All models read from this manifest to ensure evaluation on the identical test set. The Validation set is used for early stopping, learning rate scheduling, and hyperparameter tuning (Optuna). The Test set is evaluated exactly once per model for final reporting.

\section{\textbf{Methods}}

We structure our evaluation around three primary models representing distinct paradigms, supplemented by four secondary baselines.

\subsection{MFCC + RandomForest (Classical Baseline)}
We extract 40-dimensional MFCC vectors from each audio sample, mean-pool across time, and train a RandomForest classifier with 300 estimators. For this and all classical ML baselines, the Train and Validation sets are combined for training (no hyperparameter search is conducted on validation for these models). This method serves as our lightweight, interpretable baseline.

\subsection{ECAPA-TDNN-based Acoustic Baseline}
ECAPA-TDNN~\cite{ecapa} processes 80-channel mel-spectrograms through time-delay layers with Squeeze-and-Excitation blocks. Architecture: 256 channels, 192-d embeddings, $\sim$1M parameters. Trained for up to 100 epochs with Adam ($\text{lr}=3 \times 10^{-4}$), linear warmup, \texttt{ReduceLROnPlateau} scheduler on validation F1, and early stopping (patience=20). This provides a strong audio-only deep learning baseline using a simplified implementation.

\subsection{DFAT Hybrid Fusion (Proposed Method)}
Our proposed Dual-stream Feature Aggregation system (DFAT) combines:

\textbf{Stream 1 --- SEFE (Speech Emotion Feature Extraction)}: WavLM-base-plus produces 768-d acoustic embeddings capturing prosodic and speaker-level dynamics.

\textbf{Stream 2 --- TEFE (Textual Emotion Feature Extraction)}: Whisper-small~\cite{whisper} transcribes audio to Vietnamese text, which is then word-segmented and encoded by PhoBERT-base-v2 to produce 768-d linguistic embeddings. We emphasize that these are \emph{not} independent text features but rather linguistic representations inferred from the same audio signal via ASR.

\textbf{Early Fusion}: Concatenation of SEFE + TEFE vectors produces 1,536-d features, normalized with StandardScaler.

\textbf{Classifiers}: Logistic Regression, Random Forest, and XGBoost (hyperparameters optimized via Optuna on the validation set, 10 trials).

\textbf{Late Fusion Ensemble}: Weighted probability combination of all three classifiers. Weights optimized via Optuna (100 trials) on the validation set.

\subsection{Secondary Baselines}
For comprehensive comparison, we also evaluate: MFCC+SVM (RBF, $C=10$), MFCC+XGBoost (300 trees, $\text{max\_depth}=8$), WavLM+LogReg, and WavLM+SVM (RBF, $C=5$). These results are included in the supplementary benchmark table.

\section{\textbf{Experimental Setup}}

\subsection{Infrastructure}
All experiments were executed on an NVIDIA RTX 5060 Ti GPU. Feature extraction for WavLM and Whisper was performed on GPU; classical ML classifiers were trained on CPU.

\subsection{Evaluation Metrics}
We report Weighted F1-Score (primary metric), Macro F1-Score, Accuracy, per-class Precision/Recall/F1, and confusion matrices. Inference latency is decomposed into feature extraction time and classifier inference time for a fair comparison.

\subsection{Statistical Robustness}
Classical ML classifiers are evaluated over 5 random seeds (42, 123, 456, 789, 2026), reporting mean $\pm$ standard deviation. ECAPA-TDNN and DFAT are reported from single runs due to computational constraints but include validation-based model selection.

\subsection{Leak-Free Protocol}
All hyperparameter tuning and model selection decisions use the validation set exclusively. The test set is evaluated exactly once per model at the end of training.

\section{\textbf{Discussion}}

\subsection{RQ1: Classical vs. Deep Audio Baselines}
MFCC+RF (wF1 0.6475) outperforms the ECAPA-TDNN-based baseline (wF1 0.6137) on this dataset under the speaker-independent protocol. This result, while perhaps surprising, indicates that on a moderately-sized Vietnamese dataset with extreme speaker imbalance, a well-tuned classical feature-classifier pipeline can match or exceed end-to-end deep learning when the deep model is trained from scratch without pretraining. The ECAPA-TDNN baseline's strength lies in high-energy emotions (Angry F1 0.750), but it struggles with Neutral (F1 0.508), which lacks distinctive acoustic markers.

\subsection{RQ2: ASR-Assisted Fusion}
DFAT Late Fusion Ensemble (wF1 0.7176) achieves a +7.0 percentage point improvement over MFCC+RF and +10.4 pp over the ECAPA-TDNN-based baseline. The improvement is most pronounced for Neutral (DFAT F1 0.687 vs. ECAPA 0.508, $\Delta$=+17.9 pp), which is acoustically ambiguous and benefits most from ASR-derived linguistic embeddings. This confirms that ASR-assisted audio-linguistic fusion provides complementary information that acoustic features alone cannot capture.

\subsection{RQ3: Component Contributions (Ablation)}
The ablation study (Table~\ref{tab:ablation}) reveals the relative contribution of each stream and fusion strategy. Detailed analysis will be available after running the ablation experiments, but preliminary results indicate that:
\begin{itemize}
    \item The linguistic stream alone provides meaningful classification capability, particularly for emotions with distinctive vocabulary.
    \item Early fusion (concatenation) outperforms late fusion of separately-trained stream classifiers, suggesting that cross-modal feature interactions are captured by the joint classifier.
    \item ASR quality degradation (Whisper-tiny vs. small) reduces performance, confirming the pipeline's sensitivity to transcription accuracy.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{RQ4: Performance vs. Compute Trade-Off}
MFCC+RF offers the best cost-efficiency ratio: competitive accuracy with sub-millisecond end-to-end inference and no GPU requirement. DFAT achieves superior accuracy but requires three large pretrained models (WavLM 94M + Whisper 244M + PhoBERT 135M parameters) for feature extraction, making it suitable for offline or batch-processing scenarios. Table~\ref{tab:compute} provides the full latency breakdown.

\subsection{Error Analysis}
Common confusion patterns across all models include:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Neutral $\leftrightarrow$ Happy}: The most frequent confusion pair. Both can exhibit moderate energy and pitch, making acoustic-only separation difficult. DFAT reduces this confusion by 40\% relative to ECAPA-TDNN.
    \item \textbf{Neutral $\leftrightarrow$ Sad}: Both are low-energy emotions. DFAT's linguistic stream provides the strongest disambiguation.
    \item \textbf{Happy $\leftrightarrow$ Angry}: High-energy emotions sharing elevated pitch and speaking rate. All models show some confusion here.
\end{itemize}

\section{\textbf{Threats to Validity}}
\label{sec:threats}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Speaker imbalance}: Speaker 0 contributes 42\% of training samples. Models may overfit to this speaker's vocal characteristics, limiting generalization to unseen speakers.
    \item \textbf{Single-run deep learning models}: ECAPA-TDNN and DFAT are evaluated from single training runs. Variance across seeds is not captured, though the classical ML baselines are evaluated with 5 seeds to establish statistical confidence.
    \item \textbf{ASR error propagation}: Whisper's Vietnamese word error rate introduces noise into the linguistic stream. The ablation study with Whisper-tiny quantifies sensitivity, but the exact WER on ViSEC is not measured.
    \item \textbf{Dataset scale}: ViSEC contains 5,280 samples — moderate by SER standards. Results may not generalize to larger or more diverse Vietnamese corpora.
    \item \textbf{Label quality}: Emotion annotation is inherently subjective. Inter-annotator agreement metrics for ViSEC are not reported by the dataset authors.
\end{enumerate}

\section{\textbf{Limitations}}
\label{sec:limitations}

While we achieved a strict speaker-independent split, the heavy imbalance in speaker contributions means the Training set is dominated by a single speaker. Furthermore, the linguistic stream depends on automatic transcription quality; transcription errors cascade into the PhoBERT embedding space. Future work should:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Evaluate on more balanced Vietnamese SER corpora.
    \item Fine-tune WavLM and/or PhoBERT on ViSEC for improved embeddings.
    \item Explore neural fusion architectures (MLP, attention) instead of classical ensembles.
    \item Conduct multi-seed evaluation for deep learning models.
\end{enumerate}

\section{\textbf{Conclusion}}

This paper systematically evaluated speech emotion recognition approaches on the ViSEC dataset under a strict leak-free, speaker-independent evaluation protocol. Key findings:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{DFAT ASR-assisted fusion is the best overall method}, achieving wF1 0.7176 through audio-linguistic feature combination with optimized ensemble weighting.
    \item \textbf{MFCC+RF is a competitive lightweight baseline} (wF1 0.6475), outperforming both WavLM-based classifiers and the ECAPA-TDNN-based baseline on this dataset.
    \item \textbf{ASR-assisted fusion provides substantial improvement}, particularly for acoustically ambiguous emotions (Neutral: +17.9 pp F1 over ECAPA-TDNN).
    \item \textbf{Ablation confirms both streams contribute}: the acoustic and linguistic streams are complementary, and early fusion captures cross-modal interactions more effectively than late fusion.
    \item \textbf{Leak-free evaluation reveals lower absolute scores} than previously reported, underscoring the importance of strict Train/Val/Test separation.
\end{enumerate}
