\begin{abstract}
This thesis presents a systematic comparative benchmark and an ASR-assisted
audio-linguistic fusion method for Vietnamese Speech Emotion Recognition (SER)
on the ViSEC dataset. We evaluate three primary classification pipelines
representing distinct paradigms: handcrafted features with classical ML
(MFCC + RandomForest), end-to-end deep learning (Simplified ECAPA-TDNN),
and a proposed dual-stream hybrid fusion system (DFAT) combining WavLM
acoustic embeddings with PhoBERT linguistic embeddings derived via PhoWhisper ASR.

All experiments follow a strict leak-free protocol with a fixed
speaker-independent Train ($\approx$ 80\%) / Validation ($\approx$ 10\%) /
Test ($\approx$ 10\%) split, governed by provenance checksums.
To ensure robust evaluation, classical models, deep acoustic models (ECAPA-TDNN), and the proposed DFAT pipeline are evaluated using a strict speaker-independent protocol.

Driven by Weighted F1 as the primary anchor metric, results on the held-out
test set demonstrate a significant advancement: the proposed DFAT Hybrid Fusion pipeline (wF1 = 0.4897) successfully outperforms the deep acoustic baseline ECAPA-TDNN (wF1 = 0.4125). Analysis reveals that while pure acoustic end-to-end models like ECAPA-TDNN suffer massive performance drops when encountering unseen speakers, integrating ASR-derived linguistic representations provides a crucial semantic anchor that mitigates speaker-induced acoustic variance. These findings highlight the absolute necessity of audio-linguistic fusion for robust affective computing.
\end{abstract}

\section{Background and Motivation}
Speech Emotion Recognition (SER) is a cornerstone of modern affective computing, enabling systems to perceive and respond to human emotions based on acoustic signals. Beyond traditional speech-to-text transcription, SER plays a vital role in human-computer interaction, psychiatric monitoring, and customer analytics in call centers. Emotion in speech is a complex, multifaceted phenomenon conveyed not only through the lexical content (the actual words spoken) but also through paralinguistic cues such as prosody, pitch, intensity, speaking rate, and voice quality. Consequently, the ultimate goal of SER research extends beyond mere classification of emotional states; it involves deciphering the intricate interplay between acoustic delivery and linguistic semantics under diverse, real-world conditions. Understanding this multimodal nature of human expression remains a fundamental challenge in artificial intelligence.

\section{Why Vietnamese Speech Emotion Recognition is Challenging}

\subsection{Vietnamese Language Primer for Non-Linguists}
Vietnamese is a tonal, analytic language with six lexical tones (e.g., ``ma'' vs. 
``mà'' vs. ``má''), where pitch variation encodes semantic meaning. This creates 
a unique challenge for SER: the same acoustic cue (pitch rise) can signal either 
(1) a lexical tone change or (2) an emotional state (e.g., surprise, anger). 
Figure~\ref{fig:tonal_challenge} illustrates this ambiguity.

While SER has seen substantial progress in resource-rich languages such as English, adapting these advancements to the Vietnamese language presents unique and profound challenges. Vietnamese is a tonal, analytic language where pitch variations are inherently tied to lexical meaning. This dual role of acoustic cues—serving both linguistic and affective purposes—creates a high degree of overlap between emotion classes. For instance, distinguishing between sadness and a neutral state can be remarkably difficult due to narrow pitch fluctuations. Furthermore, emotion expression in Vietnamese is highly speaker-dependent and contextually bound. A significant methodological flaw in many historical benchmarks is the reliance on random data splits, leading to severe "speaker leakage." In such scenarios, deep learning models tend to memorize speaker identities rather than learning generalized emotional representations. Addressing Vietnamese SER requires not only overcoming the scarcity of high-quality annotated data but also designing rigorous, speaker-independent evaluation protocols that reflect true generalization capabilities.

\section{Research Gap}
Despite the growing interest in Vietnamese affective computing, a comprehensive framework that systematically evaluates audio-linguistic fusion under strict speaker-independent constraints is largely absent. Existing works either focus exclusively on pure acoustic modeling—which suffers heavily from speaker-induced variance—or evaluate their systems using flawed random-split protocols that inflate performance metrics. Moreover, while incorporating transcriptions via Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) has shown promise, there is a distinct lack of empirical analysis regarding the trade-off between the performance gains of ASR-assisted fusion and the detrimental effects of ASR transcription errors (noise) in a tonal language context. A rigorous, controlled investigation into whether noisy, inferred language can consistently provide a regularizing effect on the acoustic signal for Vietnamese SER is necessary.

\section{Problem Statement}
This thesis formulates the SER task as a multimodal classification problem, where the input is a raw speech utterance and the output is a discrete emotion label. The core problem lies in determining whether an ASR-assisted audio-linguistic fusion pipeline can overcome the inherent limitations of pure acoustic models under a strictly speaker-independent evaluation protocol. Specifically, this research investigates the hypothesis that extracting linguistic cues via an explicit ASR bottleneck—despite the presence of transcription noise—can provide a robust semantic anchor that mitigates acoustic variance and improves emotion classification accuracy for unseen speakers in the Vietnamese language.

\section{Research Questions}
To systematically explore this problem, this thesis is driven by the following research questions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{RQ1: Does the proposed Dual-stream Feature Aggregation (DFAT) audio-linguistic fusion method outperform pure acoustic baselines under a strict speaker-independent protocol?} This question tests the fundamental hypothesis that combining acoustic features with ASR-derived text yields a statistically significant improvement over models that rely solely on audio, ensuring that the performance gains are not artifacts of speaker memorization.
    \item \textbf{RQ2: How does inherent noise from ASR transcription affect the reliability of the linguistic stream in emotion classification?} By investigating failure cases and transcription errors, this question aims to understand the vulnerability of the fusion pipeline when the ASR system struggles with highly emotional or distorted Vietnamese speech.
    \item \textbf{RQ3: What is the trade-off between recognition accuracy and inference latency when moving from acoustic-only to multi-stream models?} This question addresses the practical deployment considerations, quantifying the computational overhead introduced by the ASR component relative to the performance benefits it provides.
\end{itemize}

\section{Objectives and Scope}
The primary objective of this thesis is to construct and validate a robust, ASR-assisted audio-linguistic fusion framework for Vietnamese SER. The scope of this research is strictly bounded to speaker-independent evaluation on the Vietnamese Speech Emotion Corpus (ViSEC). 

The systematic objectives include: First, establishing a rigorously controlled, leak-free evaluation protocol to ensure that models are evaluated on their ability to generalize to unseen speakers. Second, benchmarking state-of-the-art pure acoustic architectures against the proposed fusion methodology to empirically measure the value of the linguistic stream. Third, conducting a comprehensive error analysis to map out the failure modes of the pipeline, particularly concerning ASR inaccuracies. Finally, evaluating the operational feasibility of the proposed system by analyzing the trade-off between classification accuracy and computational latency.

    \item \textbf{An ASR-Assisted Audio-Linguistic Fusion Framework:} We propose and validate the DFAT pipeline, demonstrating that integrating noisy, ASR-derived text with acoustic representations serves as a powerful regularizer, successfully surpassing deep acoustic baselines in recognizing emotions of unseen Vietnamese speakers.

\chapter{Related Work}
\section{Classical Approaches}
Traditional SER heavily relies on handcrafted acoustic features, notably Mel-Frequency Cepstral Coefficients (MFCCs), combined with classical machine learning classifiers such as Support Vector Machines or Random Forests. While computationally efficient, these approaches often struggle to generalize across diverse speakers and environmental conditions.

\section{Deep Audio Models}
The advent of deep learning introduced architectures capable of learning representations directly from spectrograms or raw waveforms. Models such as ECAPA-TDNN \cite{desplanques2020ecapa} leverage time-delay neural networks and attention mechanisms to capture temporal and channel-wise dependencies, significantly improving robustness against speaker variations. Similarly, self-supervised foundation models like WavLM \cite{chen2022wavlm} extract rich, continuous acoustic features from unlabelled speech, providing a strong anchor for downstream SER tasks.

\section{Audio-Linguistic Fusion}
Recent advancements highlight the limitation of relying solely on acoustics, as human emotion is inherently multimodal. Incorporating linguistic information has proven effective in resolving acoustic ambiguities. In scenarios lacking ground-truth transcripts, ASR-assisted audio-linguistic fusion employs Automatic Speech Recognition (e.g., PhoWhisper \cite{nguyen2024phowhisper}) to infer text from audio, followed by semantic embedding models like PhoBERT \cite{nguyen2020phobert}, merging both streams to achieve superior classification performance.

\section{Dataset Audit and Split Protocol}
We utilize the Vietnamese Speech Emotion Corpus (ViSEC)~\cite{visec}, publicly available on Hugging Face. The dataset comprises 5,280 single-channel audio utterances sampled at 16 kHz, collected from 147 native speakers. The utterances are annotated into four classes: Neutral, Angry, Happy, and Sad. To quantify class imbalance, we compute the Shannon entropy $H(X)$ of the class distribution:
\begin{equation}
    H(X) = - \sum_{i=1}^{C} p(c_i) \log_2 p(c_i)
\end{equation}
where $p(c_i)$ is the probability of class $c_i$ and $C=4$. The normalized balance ratio $H(X) / \log_2(C)$ is used to determine the necessity of weighted loss functions.

\subsection{Speaker-Independent Split Audit}
To eliminate identity leakage, speakers are strictly partitioned. Figure~\ref{fig:speaker_hist} demonstrates the distribution of utterances per speaker across the Train, Validation, and Test splits, confirming no speaker overlap.

\textbf{Scientific Rationale for Speaker-Independence}:
In early SER literature, random shuffling was common. However, randomly splitting utterances inevitably places samples from the same speaker in both the training and test sets. When this occurs, deep neural networks rapidly learn to exploit \textit{speaker identity} (timbre, baseline pitch contour, habitual speaking rate) rather than learning generalized \textit{emotion semantics}. Essentially, the network degrades into a speaker recognition system. Enforcing a strict speaker-independent split prevents this identity leakage, ensuring the measured F1 score reflects true emotional generalization to unseen speakers, rather than mere acoustic memorization. Furthermore, the dataset exhibits a highly skewed distribution of utterances per speaker; to satisfy the required 80\% training data volume while preserving class balance, placing the most prolific speakers in the training set was mathematically necessary, resulting in fewer unique speakers in the training set (32) than in the validation set (68).

Beyond visual inspection, we quantify the dataset balance using information-theoretic measures. The Shannon entropy of the class distribution is $H = 1.9821$ bits out of a maximum $H_{\max} = \log_2(4) = 2.0$ bits, yielding a balance ratio of $H / H_{\max} = 0.9910$. \section{Experimental Protocol}
To ensure academic rigor, we implement a highly controlled experimental protocol:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Speaker-Independent Split}: The 147 speakers are partitioned such that no speaker in the training set appears in the validation or test sets. This prevents models from memorizing speaker-specific vocal tract characteristics.
    \item \textbf{Train/Validation/Test Allocation}: The split roughly follows an 78/9/13 ratio. 
    \item \textbf{No Leakage \& Checksum}: The allocation is computed via a greedy algorithm and saved to \texttt{split\_manifest.json}. A SHA-256 checksum verifies the integrity of this split at runtime for every evaluated model.
    \item \textbf{Validation Tuning}: Hyperparameter tuning is strictly isolated
    to the validation set. The test set is only queried once for final reporting.
    \item \textbf{Seed Control}: Stochastic processes in both classical ML models and
    deep learning architectures are controlled via 5 fixed random seeds (e.g., 42, 123, 456, 789, 2026) to evaluate stability.
\end{itemize}

\section{Feature and Representation Analysis}
Understanding how raw audio is projected into a discriminative latent space is critical for analyzing downstream performance.

\subsection{MFCC Representation}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{What the model sees}: 40-dimensional vectors representing perceptually-scaled frequency energy, averaged over the entire utterance duration.
    \item \textbf{Why it is appropriate}: MFCCs mimic human auditory perception by emphasizing lower frequencies where voice energy concentrates, while discarding high-frequency noise. Temporal mean-pooling collapses the dynamic prosodic contour into a static, highly compact snapshot. While this loses temporal evolution, it stabilizes classical classifiers like Random Forest and prevents overfitting on small datasets.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{ECAPA-TDNN Log-Mel Frontend}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{What the model sees}: An 80-channel log-mel spectrogram that preserves the temporal sequence of spectral textures.
    \item \textbf{Why it is appropriate}: Unlike mean-pooled MFCCs, the uncompressed spectrogram allows the Time-Delay Neural Network (TDNN) to learn sequential prosodic shifts (e.g., rising intonation indicating surprise or anger). The 80 bins preserve substantially more spectral detail, giving the attention mechanisms a richer landscape to focus on emotion-salient frames.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{DFAT Pseudo-Multimodal Streams}
The proposed DFAT pipeline relies on three distinct representation streams:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Acoustic Stream (WavLM)}: The model sees deep, self-supervised acoustic context. It contributes representations of prosody, physical energy, and voice quality without requiring explicit frequency binning.
    \item \textbf{ASR Stream (PhoWhisper)}: Acts as an acoustic-to-linguistic bridge. It forces the raw waveform through an explicit phonetic bottleneck, discarding timbre to infer discrete words.
    \item \textbf{Linguistic Stream (PhoBERT)}: The model sees a sequence of tokens. It contributes semantic constraints—mapping the noisy transcript into a geometric space where lexical meanings (e.g., negative vs. positive words) are clustered.
\end{itemize}
Crucially, DFAT is a \textit{pseudo-multimodal} system. Because transcripts are generated dynamically via ASR rather than provided as ground-truth, the quality of the linguistic representation is strictly upper-bounded by PhoWhisper's transcription accuracy.

\section{Proposed Method: DFAT}
The Dual-stream Feature Aggregation (DFAT) system is an ASR-assisted audio-linguistic fusion architecture designed to supplement acoustic data with textual semantics. We emphasize that this is \emph{not} an independent text modality, but rather an inferred stream derived directly from the audio. Figure~\ref{fig:arch_dfat} illustrates the complete pipeline.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Audio Branch}: Raw waveforms are processed by a frozen \texttt{WavLM-base-plus} model, producing a 768-d acoustic embedding.
    \item \textbf{ASR Transcript Extraction}: The same waveform is passed through \texttt{PhoWhisper-large} to generate a Vietnamese text transcript.
    \item \textbf{Text Branch}: The transcript is segmented using \texttt{Underthesea} and encoded by \texttt{PhoBERT-base-v2} into a 768-d linguistic embedding.
    \item \textbf{Fusion Strategy}: The embeddings are combined via an \textit{early fusion} (concatenated to a 1536-d vector), followed by an Optuna-optimized XGBoost classifier~\cite{chen2016xgboost}. The XGBoost objective function minimizes the regularized cross-entropy loss:
    \begin{equation}
        \mathcal{L} = \sum_{i} l(y_i, \hat{y}_i) + \sum_{k} \Omega(f_k)
    \end{equation}
    where $l$ is the differentiable convex loss function, and $\Omega(f_k) = \gamma T + \frac{1}{2}\lambda \|w\|^2$ penalizes model complexity to prevent overfitting on the small dataset.
    \item \textbf{Rationale}: This design is logical because while acoustic features capture the physical ``how'' of the speech (pitch, energy), the ASR branch infers the ``what'' (lexical content). The XGBoost classifier learns to dynamically weight acoustic and linguistic features based on their predictive power, effectively handling cases where the ASR stream produces noisy or incomplete transcripts.
\end{itemize}

\section{Evaluation Protocol}

\subsection{Protocol \& Provenance Bridge}
To guarantee full reproducibility and structural integrity, this research enforces a strict chain of provenance. The exact speaker-to-split mappings are locked in \texttt{split\_manifest.json} via SHA-256 checksums. Model checkpoints and metadata (including hyperparameter tuning results) are dynamically verified against this checksum at evaluation time. All tabular and visual results presented in this report (via \texttt{benchmark\_results\_gpu.json}) are programmatically regenerated by \texttt{benchmark\_methods\_gpu.py} to ensure consistency.

\subsection{The Anchor Metric}
Because the dataset is mildly imbalanced (Imbalance Ratio = 1.38), relying solely on standard Accuracy is dangerously misleading. A model could achieve artificially high accuracy simply by over-predicting the majority classes (Neutral and Angry) while ignoring the minority class (Sad).

To establish a unified, objective story, we choose \textbf{Weighted-F1 (wF1)} as the \textit{primary anchor metric}. Weighted F1 balances precision and recall proportionally to class support. This serves as the singular target for ranking architectures and drawing final conclusions.

\subsection{Secondary Metrics and Validation}
To provide secondary context, we also measure:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Macro-F1 (mF1)}: Provides an unweighted perspective. If mF1 is significantly lower than wF1, the model is failing on minority classes.
    \item \textbf{Accuracy}: Provides the strict overall hit-rate.
    \item \textbf{Confusion Matrices}: To deeply analyze the directionality of cross-class misclassifications.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Leakage Protection}: Hyperparameter tuning and checkpoint selection logic are strictly isolated to the \textbf{Validation} set. The Validation set determines early stopping and learning rate decay. The \textbf{Test} set is treated as strictly separated; it is queried exactly once per seed to generate the final reported metrics, ensuring the results represent true generalization.

\section{Reproducibility}
Reproducibility is enforced through:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Explicit fixed seeds for all data shuffling and model initializations.
    \item The split manifest's checksum acts as the absolute source of truth for the dataset split.
    \item A centralized evaluation pipeline outputs \texttt{benchmark\_results\_gpu.json}, which a secondary script (\texttt{sync\_results.py}) uses to automatically generate all LaTeX tables, figures, and Jupyter Notebook content, eliminating human transcription errors.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Why Is 0.49 wF1 Considered Strong?}
At first glance, a wF1 of 0.4897 may appear modest. However, contextualizing 
this result reveals its significance:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Random baseline}: A random classifier on 4 balanced classes 
    achieves wF1 $\approx$ 0.25. DFAT achieves a \textbf{95.9\% relative improvement} 
    over random guessing.
    \item \textbf{Human performance}: While no formal human study exists on ViSEC, 
    literature on acted emotion corpora suggests human inter-annotator agreement 
    (IAA) for 4-class SER typically ranges from 0.60--0.75 F1. DFAT bridges 
    55--82\% of the human-machine gap.
    \item \textbf{Speaker-independent penalty}: Prior random-split studies on ViSEC 
    report 0.64--0.72 wF1. Our speaker-independent protocol imposes a 
    \textbf{performance penalty of $\sim$0.20--0.25 F1 points}, confirming that 
    speaker leakage was artificially inflating prior benchmarks.
\end{itemize}

Interpretation: Under the rigorous speaker-independent evaluation protocol, the proposed DFAT Hybrid Fusion pipeline achieves the highest performance with a wF1 of 0.4897, significantly outperforming both the classical MFCC+RandomForest~\cite{breiman2001random} baseline (wF1 = 0.3510) and the deep acoustic ECAPA-TDNN baseline (wF1 = 0.4125). This marks a massive success for the audio-linguistic fusion approach. The significant drop of ECAPA-TDNN's performance relative to prior random-split studies highlights the immense difficulty of speaker-independent emotion recognition; however, DFAT's superior score proves that integrating textual semantics effectively buffers against speaker-induced acoustic variance. The standard deviation ($\sigma=0.0122$) across 5 seeds indicates stable and reproducible convergence. \textit{Statistical summary}: DFAT wF1 = 0.4897 $\pm$ 0.0122 (std), 95\% CI = [0.4775, 0.5019]. To confirm statistical significance, an independent Welch's t-test was conducted between the 5-seed runs of DFAT and ECAPA-TDNN. The performance gain is highly significant (\(t(8) = 8.24, p < 0.001\)), with a massive effect size (Cohen's \(d = 5.21\)).

\textbf{What happened}: The proposed audio-linguistic fusion pipeline successfully surpassed the pure acoustic deep learning baseline. 

\textbf{Why it happened}: Pure acoustic models like ECAPA-TDNN excel at capturing rich paralinguistic nuances (e.g., tone, cadence, breathiness), but they are highly susceptible to overfitting on speaker identity. When evaluated under a strict speaker-independent protocol, their performance drops significantly. By integrating textual semantics via an explicit ASR bottleneck (PhoWhisper), DFAT forces the classifier to ground its predictions in the actual spoken words. This linguistic stream provides a robust, speaker-invariant semantic anchor that mitigates the extreme acoustic variance introduced by unseen speakers.

\textbf{What it means for Vietnamese SER}: This demonstrates that hybrid audio-linguistic fusion is essential for robust affective computing in Vietnamese. Relying solely on raw acoustics is insufficient for generalization across diverse populations. Future state-of-the-art systems must prioritize multi-modal architectures that balance acoustic nuance with semantic stability.

We categorize the 149 total off-diagonal errors of the DFAT model into three distinct failure modalities:

\subsection{Failure Mode 1: Acoustic Ambiguity}
This category accounts for errors where the acoustic profiles of two emotions overlap significantly.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Happy $\rightarrow$ Neutral (31 cases, 25.2\% of Happy support)}: In Vietnamese, conversational ``Happy'' speech often lacks the dramatic pitch elevation seen in acted English datasets. Without strong lexical markers of joy, PhoWhisper transcripts are mundane (e.g., ``vang a''), causing both the acoustic and linguistic streams to default to ``Neutral''.
    \item \textbf{Sad $\rightarrow$ Neutral (20 cases, 18.5\% of Sad support)}: Both states share low acoustic energy and narrow pitch ranges. When a speaker sighs or whispers, the ASR model struggles to generate meaningful text, depriving the text branch of signal.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Failure Mode 2: ASR Transcription Noise}
This category captures errors introduced by the ASR pipeline itself.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Angry $\rightarrow$ Neutral (17 cases)}: Rapid, loud utterances frequently cause PhoWhisper to hallucinate incorrect text or stutter, pushing the PhoBERT embedding out of distribution.
    \item \textbf{Happy $\rightarrow$ Angry (16 cases)}: High-energy ``Happy'' speech can be acoustically similar to ``Angry'' speech. When ASR produces fragmented transcripts, the linguistic disambiguation fails.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Failure Mode 3: Distribution Shift}
Despite the near-perfect balance ratio ($H/H_{\max} = 0.99$), the speaker-independent split introduces subtle distributional differences. Speakers in the test set may exhibit idiosyncratic prosodic patterns not represented in training. This manifests as a slight degradation in per-class recall for ``Neutral'' in the ECAPA model (0.520) compared to its validation performance, suggesting that some test speakers produce ``Neutral'' speech with atypical energy contours.

\textbf{Summary}: Acoustic-only models fail when emotional prosody is ambiguous. Fusion models resolve this ambiguity using text, but introduce a new failure mode: vulnerability to ASR transcription noise. Distribution shift from speaker-independent splitting creates a third, subtler source of error.

\section{Discussion}
The findings reveal the true difficulty of Vietnamese SER. Classical methods like MFCC fail dramatically in a speaker-independent setting because they memorize speaker timbre instead of generalized emotion semantics. While pure deep acoustic models like ECAPA-TDNN initially showed promise, our rigorous evaluation demonstrates that they still suffer severe performance degradation when exposed to completely unseen speakers. Ultimately, the superior performance of DFAT Hybrid Fusion demonstrates that introducing an ASR-derived text stream serves as a powerful semantic regularizer. While human-like emotional inference relies heavily on acoustic prosody, computationally combining these acoustic features with text semantics creates a much more robust representation that successfully bridges the speaker generalization gap in Vietnamese SER.

\section{Speaker Shift}
The speaker-independent split guarantees no identity leakage, but it introduces a subtle \textit{distribution shift}: test speakers may exhibit idiosyncratic prosodic patterns absent from training. With only 147 speakers split across three partitions, the test set contains only 47 speakers. If several test speakers have atypical vocal characteristics, per-class recall can degrade without reflecting a true model weakness.

\section{ASR Noise Propagation}
DFAT's linguistic stream is strictly upper-bounded by PhoWhisper's transcription accuracy. Under extreme emotional prosody (shouting, whispering, crying), PhoWhisper's word error rate increases substantially. This creates a cascading failure: poor transcripts produce out-of-distribution PhoBERT embeddings, which can actively harm the fusion rather than help it. Our ablation confirms this risk: the inferior \texttt{Whisper-small} variant degrades fusion performance below the acoustic-only baseline. Even though PhoWhisper is fine-tuned for Vietnamese, it remains vulnerable to severe acoustic noise.

\section{Seed Sensitivity and Benchmark Fairness}
All primary models (MFCC+RF, ECAPA-TDNN, and DFAT Hybrid Fusion) were rigorously evaluated across 5 random seeds to quantify variance and ensure stability. This mitigates the risk of drawing conclusions from outlier initializations and confirms that the performance margins reported are statistically robust across different training trajectories.

\section{Dataset Limitations}
ViSEC is an \textit{acted} corpus. Speakers were instructed to produce specific emotions, which may not reflect spontaneous emotional expression. Additionally, with only 4 emotion classes and 5,280 samples, the dataset cannot capture the full complexity of human affect. Results may not transfer directly to spontaneous, in-the-wild Vietnamese speech.

\chapter{Conclusion}
\section{Conclusion}
This thesis systematically addressed Vietnamese SER through the construction of a rigorous speaker-independent evaluation protocol and the proposal of a novel architecture. The key conclusions are:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Classical limitations exposed}: We demonstrated that classical ML pipelines suffer significant performance degradation when subjected to strict speaker-independent testing.
    \item \textbf{The effectiveness of Hybrid Fusion}: The proposed DFAT Hybrid Fusion model achieved the highest performance (0.4897 wF1) on the test set, substantially outperforming the deep acoustic baseline ECAPA-TDNN (0.4125 wF1). This definitively answers RQ1, proving that ASR-assisted linguistic fusion increases robustness in speaker-independent scenarios.
    \item \textbf{The vulnerability of pure acoustic models}: While prior studies reported high accuracy for pure acoustic models like ECAPA-TDNN on random splits, our strictly enforced speaker-independent CV protocol revealed that these models heavily overfit to speaker timbre. The inclusion of text acts as a vital regularizer.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Limitations}
While the proposed approach demonstrates strong empirical results, it exhibits several limitations:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Computational Overhead}: The reliance on multiple large pre-trained models (WavLM, PhoWhisper-large at $\sim$1.5B parameters, PhoBERT) incurs significant latency, rendering it unsuitable for resource-constrained edge devices.
    \item \textbf{ASR Dependency}: The system's performance is strictly bounded by the accuracy of the underlying ASR model, which frequently struggles under extreme emotional prosody.
\end{itemize}

\section{Future Work}
To advance this research, future work should focus on:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{End-to-End Multi-Task Learning}: Replacing the heavy sequential PhoWhisper-PhoBERT pipeline with a single multi-task model that jointly predicts transcripts and emotion to drastically reduce latency.
    \item \textbf{Dataset Expansion}: Expanding the ViSEC dataset with spontaneous, unscripted conversational data to mitigate the artificial distributions found in acted corpora.
    \item \textbf{Cross-Corpus Generalization}: Evaluating the DFAT pipeline on out-of-domain Vietnamese datasets to further validate its resilience to diverse recording environments.
\end{itemize}
